Scoring criteria

How numbers in this lab are meant to be read. Heuristic educational model — not a validated clinical scale.

CBI estimates load balance and accumulation risk. It does not estimate human worth, identity, or diagnosis.

1. Factor scale (0–1)

Each process factor is a continuous exposure from 0 (little/none of this load) to 1 (severe/persistent of this load). Recovery opportunity is inverted into recovery deficit in the math.

  • 0.00–0.25 — low or rare
  • 0.25–0.50 — moderate / intermittent
  • 0.50–0.75 — high / frequent
  • 0.75–1.00 — very high / near-constant

Verbal anchors under key sliders define what those levels mean for that factor.

2. Questionnaire (1–10 → factor)

Each item is rated 1 (not like me) to 10 (very much like me). Conversion:

value = (rating − 1) / 9 → maps to 0–1.

Inverted items (e.g. “I get real off-time…”) flip: high agreement lowers load. Multiple items for the same factor are averaged. Unanswered items are skipped (not treated as zero).

3. Static CBI (0–100)

Weighted sum of process factors, with demand factors amplified by low controllability:

D* = D × (1 + γ × low_control), γ ≈ 0.45

Then other body/context/recovery-deficit terms add. Result scaled to 0–100 and clamped.

Weights are documented heuristics (see Mathematics), optionally retuned slightly by Big Five sensitivity — not moral scores.

4. Zones (barometer)

RangeZoneReading
0–25Adaptive / lightDemand absorbable if recovery holds
26–50Adaptive / workingReal operational stress; still constructive if recovery & agency keep pace
51–75Erosive riskProlonged imbalance — early intervention window
76–100Toxic loadUnchecked erosion likely — prioritize load cut + recovery + controllability

5. Dynamic score & trajectory

Latent load L updates from day to day: rises with high load, falls with recovery opportunity. Dynamic CBI = 100 × L / (L + K).

  • Accumulating — multi-day path climbs (> ~5 points)
  • Stable — little net change
  • Recovering — path declines

Trajectory is as important as today’s level: toxic stress is often a run, not a single peak.

6. Top drivers & %

Each bar is that factor’s share of the current weighted total (after amplification). Top 6 are process drivers for attention — not identity labels, not diagnoses.

7. Day log scoring

Each logged day contributes a compact load/recovery signal that steps L. Use it to see whether real weeks accumulate or ease — still illustrative, not medical monitoring.

8. What scores are not

  • Not a diagnosis or symptom inventory score
  • Not a ranking of people, groups, or identities
  • Not proof of resilience or failure
  • Not a substitute for clinical, legal, or emergency help

9. Blind spots

This model does not adequately capture acute trauma, ongoing violence, severe psychiatric emergency, untreated major medical disease, or deep deprivation. If those are present, real-world support comes first. In the US, crisis line: 988.
