Big Five (OCEAN) & Allostatic Load

Trait descriptors and hypothetical correlations with cognitive burden and allostatic accumulation. Used in this lab only as sensitivity knobs — not as clinical ratings.

Ethical framing. The Big Five are descriptive trait dimensions from personality research. High or low scores are not disorders. Links to stress load are probabilistic and context-dependent. This page supports personal exploration of an evolving model, not assessment of others.

O — Openness

Curiosity, aesthetic sensitivity, preference for novelty and abstraction.

Load notes: May seek complex work (raising WM) but tolerate ambiguity better. Weak direct link to allostatic load in most studies; context matters.

C — Conscientiousness

Orderliness, industriousness, reliability, planfulness.

Load notes: Often protective via better pacing and fewer self-inflicted deadlines — yet can enable compensatory overwork. In the CBI toy model, higher C slightly reduces task-switching weight.

E — Extraversion

Sociability, assertiveness, positive activation, reward sensitivity.

Load notes: Social interruptions may cost less; solitary recovery may cost more if preferred recovery is social. Model: higher E slightly lowers interruption weight.

A — Agreeableness

Compassion, politeness, cooperation versus antagonism.

Load notes: High A may increase exposure to emotional labor and difficulty asserting recovery boundaries; not coded as a hard weight shift in the current calculator (room to extend).

N — Neuroticism

Tendency toward negative emotion, volatility, and threat sensitivity.

Load notes: Most consistent Big Five correlate of stress reactivity and many mental-health risk pathways. In the model, higher N amplifies weights on emotional load, sleep disruption, low controllability, and recovery deficit — a sensitivity hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

Adaptation to the evolving CBI model

Traits act as modifiers of cost, not as extra demand scores. Two people in the same workplace profile can show different CBI paths if $N$ or $E$ differ. That matches the broader idea of stress–vulnerability without claiming clinical prediction.

Suggested reading order with MBPA

Big Five = continuous trait research language · MBPA/16 types = preference typology language. They are not the same system. Use Big Five for sensitivity in the calculator; use MBPA for workplace-style reflection on preferred modes of attention and decision. Neither replaces the demand/recovery structure of the CBI itself.
