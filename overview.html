Overview

Core ideas behind mental ergonomics, allostatic cognitive load, and the Cognitive Burden Index.

Mental Ergonomics

The design of cognitive work so that attention, memory, and decision-making are used efficiently and sustainably. Poor mental ergonomics creates unnecessary switching, overload, and error.

Allostatic Load

The cumulative “wear and tear” that results from repeated adaptation to demand. When recovery does not keep pace with load, allostatic load rises — first cognitively, then physiologically.

Cognitive Burden Index

A composite score (0–100) that estimates present cognitive burden from measurable or estimable factors. It is designed to be transparent, adjustable, and educational — not a clinical diagnosis.

Adaptive vs toxic stress

Stress is neutral. Demand with recovery, agency, and meaning can produce growth. The same demand without recovery, controllability, or support becomes erosive — especially when prolonged. This lab exists to surface factors early enough that strategies can keep load adaptive and prevent long-term harm.

Why combine them?

Cognitive Load Theory focuses on the structure of the task and the design of instruction or interfaces. Allostatic load focuses on what happens when demand is repeated without adequate recovery. Mental ergonomics sits between them: it asks how to shape cognitive work so that burden stays within sustainable bounds.

The Cognitive Burden Index is a practical instrument for that conversation. It forces the factors we care about into the open, assigns them explicit weights, and makes the difference between a high-demand moment and a high-demand day visible.
