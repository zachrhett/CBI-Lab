MBPA — 16 Personalities Breakdown

Educational descriptors of the sixteen preference types used in Myers-Briggs–style (MBPA) frameworks, with optional notes on how cognitive burden factors may interact with those preferences.

Prefer an interactive pass first? Take the MBPA preference test (20 forced-choice items → 4-letter sketch + load notes).
Framing. Personality type labels are preference patterns, not diagnoses, abilities, or fixed destinies. They do not predict mental illness. Links to CBI factors below are correlational hypotheses for reflection and workplace design — individuals vary widely within every type.

The four preference pairs

E — Extraversion vs I — Introversion

Where attention and energy tend to be directed: outward interaction and breadth, or inward focus and depth.

S — Sensing vs N — Intuition

How information is preferably taken in: concrete facts and present detail, or patterns, possibilities, and abstraction.

T — Thinking vs F — Feeling

How decisions are preferably made: impersonal criteria and logical consistency, or values and impact on people.

J — Judging vs P — Perceiving

How lifestyle and work are preferably organized: planned and settled, or flexible and open to emerging options.

MBPA attributes × allostatic load (correlational sketch)

Hypothetical mappings for personal model-building. Preference patterns may change which CBI inputs feel most costly and which recovery strategies restore latent load faster — not who gets ill.

Energy attitude (E / I)

I: interruption density and open collaborative load often raise effective burden faster; solitary recovery helps more.

E: isolation plus high demand can fail as recovery; social recovery may work better if emotional load is not hostile.

Information (S / N)

S: ambiguous priorities and missing concrete standards raise working-memory and controllability costs.

N: repetitive high-switching operational work without conceptual grip can elevate emotional load and detachment failure.

Decision (T / F)

T: value-conflict-heavy roles may under-weight emotional labor until human impact backlog appears.

F: display rules, aggression exposure, and care intensity map strongly onto emotional load and recovery need.

Lifestyle (J / P)

J: unfinished-task carryover and role ambiguity are potent drivers of perseveration and sleep disruption.

P: rigid surveillance and zero recovery under fixed deadlines stress time pressure plus low control.

Using types with the Cognitive Burden Index

Preference is not destiny. A useful practice is to ask which CBI factors are structurally high in the role and which clash with preferred modes of working:

  • I preferences often report higher cost from interruption density and open-office switching.
  • N preferences may under-specify concrete steps when working memory demand is already high.
  • F preferences can carry heavier emotional load in conflict-intense or display-rule jobs.
  • J preferences may experience unfinished-task carryover and backlog pressure as especially aversive.
  • P preferences may absorb switching better but underperform when recovery opportunity is zero and external deadlines are rigid.

These are design prompts for mental ergonomics, not scoring rules for people.
